City Council Approves New Riverside Park

By Jordan Avery, March 3, 2025

The city council voted seven to two on Tuesday night to approve a new riverside park that will turn a stretch of abandoned warehouses along the eastern bank into twelve acres of public green space, walking trails and a community boat launch.

Supporters of the plan, which has been debated for nearly four years, said the park would give residents of the surrounding neighborhoods their first direct access to the river in generations. Several speakers at the public hearing described growing up only a few blocks from the water without ever being able to reach it.

The project is expected to cost about eighteen million dollars, most of which will come from a state grant awarded last autumn. The remaining funds will be drawn from the city's capital improvement budget over the next three fiscal years, according to a summary presented by the finance director.

Opponents raised concerns about long-term maintenance costs and the loss of potential tax revenue from redeveloping the warehouses for housing. One council member argued that the city should first address the backlog of repairs in its existing parks before committing to a new one.

Construction is scheduled to begin next spring, with the first section of the trail expected to open to the public by the end of next year. The city will hold a series of design workshops this summer to gather input on playgrounds, seating and lighting.
